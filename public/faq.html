FAQ

Questions, answered plainly.

What Spawn is, where it runs, how early access works, and what happens when you put an app on the internet.

What is Spawn?

Spawn is a private app builder that runs on your machine. You create a real app, run a local preview, change it in Build chat, then Go Live for a public HTTPS URL — with domains, secrets, and plugins in the same place.

Is Spawn local or cloud?

Building stays local. Your project files, preview, and secrets live on your machine until you choose to publish. Go Live puts a public site on the internet; the builder itself is not a browser-only sandbox that holds your code.

How does early access work?

Spawn is in private beta. Join the waitlist with your email on the early access page. We invite people in waves and email when it’s your turn — no spam, no pitch deck drip.

Can I use a custom domain?

Yes. After Go Live you can attach your own hostname from inside Spawn — apex and www — without bouncing to a separate hosting console. Domains stay wired to the app you’re shipping.

Where do my code and secrets live?

Code stays on your machine until you publish. Secrets (API keys, env vars) are stored in Spawn’s Secrets and injected at run and deploy time — they don’t belong in your source tree. See Privacy for how this site handles waitlist email.

What do I need to get started?

A machine that can run Spawn, and (when invited) access to the private beta. You describe an app or open existing files, hit Run for a localhost preview, then iterate in chat. No separate deploy dance just to try a change.

How is this different from chat-only builders?

Spawn edits real project files, shows the preview on your machine, and ships a public URL with domains and secrets included. It’s a builder loop — create → run → build → live — not a one-shot chat that dumps disposable code.

Is there pricing yet?

Not publicly. Private beta first. Join early access and we’ll share plans when seats open — building in public on spawnapp.org.
